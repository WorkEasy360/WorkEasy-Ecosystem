# ADR-066: Same account or a different one - Ask once by default, Create New Account, Switch Account that reaches the central sign-in, and changing the sign-in email

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: accepted; built on `feat/partner-admin-panel`, not committed or deployed yet. Amends ADR-044 (item 14: the default preference; item 4: the access chooser's answers) and ADR-059 (a portal's sign-out is that portal's - Switch Account is the one exception, below).

## Context

The owner's brief "WorkEasy360 - Unified Platform, Shared Login & Product Access System (master development prompt, updated requirements)" restates the whole ecosystem (one identity, several organizations, several product subscriptions, SSO, three wallets, Super Admin and Partner boundaries, mobile) and adds one **mandatory** new requirement: when a person with a live WorkEasy360 session opens a product, ask "Continue with the same account" or "Use a different account" (plus "Create a new account" where registration is open), naming the signed-in account - on first entry into a product in the session, never on every page (section 5). It also asks for a separate account-security area to change the sign-in email with verification (5.6), and for Switch Account and Switch Organization in the header account menu (19).

The audit (`docs/unified-login/status.md`, section 1) found almost everything else already built: OIDC authorization code + PKCE with the identity provider on `auth.` (ADR-043/044), entitlement, seat and membership checks at the provider and re-checked by every product (ADR-044 items 6, 11), direct product URLs (item 15), the only-organization rule (16), the organization chooser, three owner-checked idempotent wallets (ADR-060/064/065), partner KYC (ADR-061), host-scoped sessions with `__Host-` cookies, sessions list/revoke, password change and reset. And an access chooser already existed - but since ADR-044 item 14 the default preference was **Automatic**, so a person who never chose was never asked. What was missing: the question by default, a "create a new account" answer, a way to change the sign-in email, Switch Account / Switch Organization in the menu, and any clearing of a previous person's browser state.

## Decision

1. **"Ask once" is a stored sign-in preference and the default** (`SignInPreference.ASK_ONCE`, `contracts.values.sign_in.DEFAULT_PREFERENCE`; `ecosystem.0006` widens the stored values and their check constraint). Decided where ADR-044 decides everything else: at the authorization endpoint, *after* membership, organization, access policy, entitlement and seat (a refusal is never hidden behind the question). `SignInDecision.asks(entered_this_sign_in=...)`: Automatic never asks; Ask once asks unless this sign-in has already entered the product; Ask each time and the product-login preference always ask. Supersedes ADR-044 item 14. **People who stored a choice keep it** - only "never chose" changes.
2. **"Entered in this sign-in" lives in the identity provider's session** (`platform.ecosystem.sign_in.SESSION_ENTERED_KEY`), written only where an account is actually chosen - a code issued at `resume`, after the chooser's answer or a sign-in for the request (`record_entry`); `authorize`'s silent path writes nothing (Automatic needs no record, and Ask once only goes silent once recorded), which keeps its query budget, and bound to the person *and* the sign-in id it was written for, so it answers for nobody else and for no later sign-in even where Django keeps a same-person session. Signing out flushes it; a different person signing in starts a new session. Never a stored preference: nothing is written to the database. A person who signs in *for* the request (the central sign-in page) has answered, and is not asked again - as before. `prompt=select_account`/`consent` still always ask; `prompt=none` gets `interaction_required` until the first entry.
3. **The chooser reads the brief's way** (`features/signin/ProductAccessChooser.tsx`): "Opening <product>", **Welcome to WorkEasy360**, "How would you like to continue?", **Currently signed in as** name, email and organization, then **Continue with Same Account** (primary), **Use Different Account**, **Create New Account** (only while sign-up is open), the product's own sign-in where it has one, "Remember my choice" / "Don't ask me again for <product>" (stores Automatic for that product), Cancel and Sign-in preferences. The data endpoint adds `choices.create_account` and nothing else.
4. **Create New Account spends the request and signs out first** (`Choice.CREATE_ACCOUNT`). Never re-parked: a brand-new account holds no entitlement, so the request could only be refused, and nothing of it - its organization least of all - may follow the browser into somebody else's sign-up. The destination is built on the server from configuration and the catalog only: the Store's sign-up with `next=/store/product/<slug>` when the Store sells the product (`catalog.selectors.store_page_slug`: listed, available, allowed on the customer Store), otherwise the plain sign-up. Refused with `signup_closed` - before the request is touched - while registration is closed. No entitlement is granted before a real purchase (brief 5.4.7).
5. **Switch Account reaches the central sign-in.** Each portal signs in on its own host (ADR-059), so a portal sign-out left the `auth.` session - the one products sign in with - alive, and the next product would have opened as the previous person. `POST auth/switch-account` (account portals; session + CSRF) signs this portal out and, in multi-portal mode, answers `https://auth.../switch-account#handle=<signed>`: a `django.core.signing` hand-over naming the person and the portal, valid 120 s, in the **fragment** (never sent to a server by a browser). That page posts it to `POST auth/switch-account/finish` (auth host only; CSRF), which ends the `auth.` session **only if it belongs to the same person**, and answers that portal's `/login?switched=1` in every case - so the endpoint is no oracle and cannot sign a stranger out (a logout-CSRF needs a handle only the victim's own CSRF-protected session can mint). Single-host mode: one sign-out, then `/login?switched=1`. Rejected: a confirmation page on `auth.` (one more click for a choice already made), a shared parent-domain cookie (ADR-043 forbids it), signing out every `auth.` session of the person (other devices).
6. **Leaving an account forgets it in the browser** (`lib/accountState.ts`). Every sign-out and switch removes the person-bound `localStorage`/`sessionStorage` keys (recent admin records, a cached partner list, carts, the Store's chosen organization and return pages, notifications seen) and keeps layout choices and what a visitor brought before signing in (referral token, purchase intent - sign-up still needs them). The customer and staff shells then leave with `location.replace`, a full page load: nothing survives in memory, and Back does not reopen the previous person's page. The server never trusted any of this; it is about the next person at the same browser seeing names.
7. **Changing the sign-in email keeps the identity** (`platform.administration.self_service.request_email_change` / `confirm_email_change`; `identity.0009`; `identity.lifecycle.change_email` keeps `User` single-writer). `POST auth/email` needs the current password (a wrong one counts toward the lockout) and mails a single-use link (`AccountToken` purpose `change_email`, hash only, `new_email` column with a database default, 24 h, the latest link only) to the new address, sealed, in the fragment. One answer whatever the address: an address another account holds is told instead ("already has an account", hourly), as signup does. `POST public/email/confirm` (the link alone, in any browser; a click, not a page load, so a mail scanner changes nothing) moves the sign-in email, marks it verified, spends every other outstanding link (a reset mailed to the old address dies), tells the old address with the new one masked (`a***@domain`), and records `email_change_requested` / `email_changed` in the person's own trail. Same id, password, sessions, memberships, wallets; the products know the person by `(issuer, sub)` (checked in Easy CRM and EasyBook provisioning), so no product account is duplicated - their stored copy of the address stays stale until they refresh it. Rate limits: 5 per person per hour, 3 per target address per hour, 20 per address per minute; confirm 20/min per address.
8. **The header account menu** (`features/launcher/Shell.tsx`): My Profile, **Switch Organization** (`/organizations/select`, which lists the person's organizations and re-checks membership on every request - the tenant is the URL path, never a client value), **Switch Account**, Account & Security, the Admin / Partner consoles where the person holds them, Logout. The menu's header names the current organization and role. Account & Security gains **Sign-in email** and **Sign-in methods** (the password; products through WorkEasy360 single sign-on with a link to the preference) - two-step verification stays off by the owner's decision (ADR-050).

## Consequences

- A person who never chose sees the chooser once per product after each sign-in, including on a product's direct URL: one extra click on first entry, which is the brief's requirement. Automatic is one setting away (and "Don't ask me again" on the chooser itself).
- Rollback: the previous release cannot read an `ask_once` row. `migrate ecosystem 0005` deletes those rows first (they exist only for people who picked Ask once explicitly; they then get that release's default). `identity.0009` is additive and safe under the previous release.
- Not done here, and why: native Android/iOS sign-in (the provider has confidential clients only - no public clients, custom-scheme redirect URIs or refresh tokens - and no native app exists in any repository; the Easy CRM Android app is a TWA, which runs the web flow and works as is); an HR product (none is registered); `books.` (EasyBook is registered as `book.workeasy360.com`). See `docs/unified-login/status.md`.
